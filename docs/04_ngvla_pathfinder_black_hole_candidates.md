# Stage 4: ngVLA Pathfinder and Black Hole Candidates

## Overview

The next-generation VLA (ngVLA) is the planned US centimeter-wave array. A July 2026 NSF–NRAO–Naval Observatory partnership announced an ngVLA pathfinder using existing VLBA stations plus new US sites, with construction before decade's end. An ngVLA memo sketches a VLBA-plus-ngVLA-Long campaign targeting faint core radio sources in GCs.

## ngVLA Pathfinder Status (as of mid-2026)

- Uses existing VLBA stations + new US sites
- Construction targeted before end of decade
- ngVLA High-resolution Frontier workshop: March 22–24, 2027, Johns Hopkins University; registration open through December 15, 2026

## Proposed VLBA + ngVLA-Long Campaign

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Target sources | 54 faint core radio sources |
| Flux density | 10–100 microjansky at 5 GHz |
| Clusters | 14 globular clusters |
| Science goal | Proper motions of candidate stellar-mass black holes |

## Scientific Rationale

Stellar-mass black holes (sMBHs) in GCs are expected to be the heaviest objects and to sink to the core, but they are dynamically hard to detect optically. Radio continuum from accretion or from a pulsar companion offers an alternative channel. A 10–100 microjansky source at GC distance (5–10 kpc) corresponds to radio luminosities consistent with low-level accretion onto sMBHs or with faint MSPs.

Proper motion measurements distinguish cluster members from background AGN. Sources with proper motions matching the cluster's systemic motion are candidate sMBHs or MSPs; mismatched motions are background contaminants.

## Connection to Prior Work

- Omega Centauri oMEGACat: 21-year HST astrometric wobble revealed oMEGACat BH-2 (~4.5 solar masses), first sMBH in any GC detected purely astrometrically (Whitaker et al. 2025)
- Radio continuum offers an independent, complementary detection channel
- GC cores are the natural hunting ground: sMBHs segregate to the center

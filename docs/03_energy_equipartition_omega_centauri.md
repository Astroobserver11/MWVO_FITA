# 03. Energy Equipartition in Omega Centauri

## 3.1 The measurement (Anderson & van der Marel 2010)

Using the 53,000 high-quality central proper motions from Stage 2, Anderson and van der Marel plotted **proper-motion velocity dispersion against stellar mass** along the main sequence of Omega Centauri.

**Prediction for full equipartition:** sigma proportional to m^(-1/2). Massive stars move slower; light stars move faster, in exact inverse-square-root proportion.

**Observation:** dispersion drops as mass increases, but the slope is **shallower than -1/2**. The cluster is in **partial equipartition**.

## 3.2 Physical interpretation

- Massive stars have sunk toward the center and slowed (mass segregation is underway).
- But the system has **not had time to erase the memory of its formation conditions**.
- Omega Centauri's present-day mass function still reflects something about how it assembled — it is not a fully relaxed, equilibrium system.
- This is consistent with Omega Centauri's suspected origin as the stripped core of a dwarf galaxy (its mass, multiple populations, and rotation all point this way), which would have a longer relaxation time than a classical globular of similar mass.

## 3.3 The other side of the coin: anisotropy (HSTPROMO Paper 2)

Watkins et al. 2015 mapped velocity **anisotropy** across 22 clusters:

- Cores are **isotropic to within 1%**.
- Radial anisotropy builds up in the outskirts as two-body encounters scatter stars onto radial orbits over a relaxation time.

## 3.4 Dynamical clock

Taken together, equipartition (mass-dependent dispersion) and anisotropy (orbit-shape evolution) give a **two-dimensional dynamical clock**:

- Isotropy in the core => center has relaxed.
- Mild radial anisotropy outward => halo is still catching up.
- Partial equipartition => the cluster is mid-relaxation, not primordial, not fully mixed.

This framework is directly relevant to the user's ISM/dusty-plasma models: embedded clusters begin far from equipartition (gas-dominated, supersonic turbulence) and evolve toward it as gas is expelled and two-body relaxation proceeds. The globular end-state (partial equipartition, isotropic core) is the asymptotic limit the dusty-plasma initial conditions must eventually approach — or be disrupted before reaching.

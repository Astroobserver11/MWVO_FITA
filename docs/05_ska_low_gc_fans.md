# Stage 5: SKA-LOW and the GC FANS Census

## Overview

SKA-LOW (50–350 MHz) is optimized for steep-spectrum radio sources. Many GC MSPs have steep spectra and are brightest at low frequencies, making SKA-LOW a natural complement to SKA-MID's higher-frequency sensitivity.

## GC FANS (FAST)

- FAST's GC FANS survey added 60 pulsars in 41 clusters (early 2025)
- 35 binaries among the 60
- FAST's sensitivity at ~1.4 GHz revealed a population fainter than prior surveys reached
- Many FAST GC pulsars are below VLBI detection thresholds — a gap SKA addresses

## SKA-LOW Specific Advantages for GC Work

1. **Steep-spectrum MSPs**: spectral index α ≈ −1.5 to −2.5; flux scales as ν^α, so low frequencies boost signal
2. **Scattering**: low frequencies suffer more scattering in the ISM, but GC lines of sight through the bulge are already scatter-dominated at high frequencies for some sources; the trade-off must be evaluated per cluster
3. **Continuum sensitivity**: SKA-LOW's wide field of view enables efficient continuum surveys of GC cores for sMBH candidates (complementing Stage 4)

## Open Questions for SKA-LOW GC Programs

- Can SKA-LOW detect the radio continuum from sMBH candidates in GC cores?
- What fraction of FAST GC pulsars have measurable proper motions with SKA-VLBI?
- Do low-frequency timing arrays (pulsar timing arrays) gain GC MSP contributions?

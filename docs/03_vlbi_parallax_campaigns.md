# Stage 3: VLBI Parallax Campaigns

## Overview

Very Long Baseline Interferometry (VLBI) provides the highest-precision astrometry available for pulsars, delivering parallax and proper motion measurements that anchor cluster distances and internal kinematics independently of optical methods.

## PSR-pi and MSPSR-pi Campaigns

- Run on the Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA)
- Over a decade of pulsar parallax measurements
- MSPSR-pi specifically targets millisecond pulsars, including GC sources
- Precision: typically 10–50 microarcseconds for bright MSPs

## SKA-VLBI with MultiView Calibration

- Projected parallax precision: ~8 microarcseconds
- Reach: ~12.5 kiloparsecs for approximately half the known pulsar sample
- MultiView calibration uses multiple calibrator sources to solve for ionospheric and tropospheric phase errors across the array
- Critical for GC work: GCs are concentrated toward the Galactic plane and bulge, where ionospheric and scattering effects are severe

## Scientific Payoff for GC Studies

1. **Independent distance ladder**: VLBI parallaxes bypass the uncertainties of photometric and kinematic distance methods
2. **Internal kinematics**: proper motions of multiple pulsars within one cluster map the gravitational potential, including possible intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) signatures
3. **Acceleration measurements**: timing plus astrometry yields line-of-sight accelerations, constraining the cluster potential independently

## Limitations

- Faint GC pulsars (many FAST discoveries are below VLBI detection thresholds)
- Ionospheric calibration near the Galactic plane
- Time baseline requirements: parallax needs ~1–2 years minimum; high precision needs longer
